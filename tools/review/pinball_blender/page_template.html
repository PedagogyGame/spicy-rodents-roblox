The three machines, drawn from the game's own numbers, so you can model each one in Blender and have it fit exactly. Facing them, SPACE SQUIRREL stands on the left, SPICY RODENT in the middle and ACORN ATTACK on the right. All three use one cabinet and differ in playfield, colours and rules.

Sizes

The game measures in studs. Your art packs are modelled in metres and imported with Scale Unit set to Meter, and Roblox counts 1 stud as 0.28 m. Multiply any stud figure by 0.28 for Blender. The drawings give both, and the blockout files are already in metres.

The playfield is a real table's 20¼ × 42 inch shape at about 1.3 times real size. Model the playfield flat: the game tilts it.

Must stay where it is

The ball's physics knows the playfield as a flat outline. Anything the ball touches must keep its position and footprint from the plans:

  • wooden rails and the arch, wire guides, lane dividers
  • posts, pop bumper bodies, slingshots
  • flippers, targets, the spinner, the gate
  • the ramp tracks and their heights

Keep heights about the same. A taller guide is fine; one lower than the ball (0.125) is not.

Yours to design

  • Bumper skirts and caps
  • Plastics above the ball, between 0.2 and 0.45 high (the glass is at 0.5)
  • Ramp side walls and their entrance flaps
  • Cabinet art, legs, backbox, lockdown bar, coin door
  • Playfield and backglass artwork

Bare or missing in the game today, marked on the plans: the SPACE SQUIRREL lock-pod hoods, ramp side walls, bumper skirts, the brackets behind standup targets.

Moving parts

Make each of these its own object with its origin at the pivot, so the game can move it.

PartHow it moves
FlippersTurn about the pivot pin (the black dot on the plans), from 30° below level to 38° above. Origin on the pin, 0.035 above the board.
Drop targetsSlide straight down 0.16 into the board when hit, and come back up together (ACORN ATTACK).
SpinnerSpins about a level axis across its lane, 0.10 above the board (ACORN ATTACK).
PlungerRod and knob slide 0.32 toward the player and spring back.
Bumper caps, slingshot rubbers, lamp insertsLight up or flash. The game recolours them, so each is a separate object.
One-way gateA thin flap that swings one way.
BallsPlain spheres, 0.125 across.

The game finds moving parts by name. Each machine's sheet below lists its names.

The cabinet

  • Body: 2.7 wide, tilted with the playfield. It reaches 0.85 below the playfield, and its side walls stand 0.55 above it.
  • On top: a chrome lockdown bar across the front, just above the glass.
  • Legs: four, 0.22 square. The front pair is 2.46 tall and the back pair 3.02, which gives the slope.
  • Front: a coin door 0.70 × 0.55 that only says FREE PLAY, a red flipper button on each side, and the plunger knob on the player's right.
  • Backbox: 2.7 wide, 3.25 tall (4.45 to 7.7 above the floor) and 0.6 deep. The game writes five lines on its face: title, score, info, a message and the best score. A light strip runs along its top.

Shared parts

  • Flippers: 0.33 from pin to tip, 0.09 across at the pin and 0.056 at the tip, 0.07 tall.
  • Posts: radius 0.022. Rubber posts are 0.11 tall and metal posts 0.14.
  • Targets: 0.03 thick and 0.12 tall.
  • Pop bumpers: radius 0.11 to 0.12 depending on the table, 0.16 tall, with a lit cap on top.
  • Walls: wooden rails 0.06 × 0.2, wire guides 0.022 × 0.11, plastic guides 0.045 × 0.1, rubber bands 0.035 × 0.08.

Open the folder on GitHub

In the playfield blockouts the origin is the playfield's front-left corner. X runs to the player's right and Y is height. Up the table is −Z, which Blender's importer turns into a normal Z-up scene.

Before it goes into the game, use plain colours or one texture per mesh, because layered material sets (SurfaceAppearance) are kept out of the published place for now. Each machine is about 140 parts today, with a ceiling of 160, so a few well-made meshes replacing many parts is welcome. Send them the way you sent the store and roof packs.
