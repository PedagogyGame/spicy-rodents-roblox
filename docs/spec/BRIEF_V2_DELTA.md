# Brief v2 delta: what changed, what the plan already covers, what must move

Status: analysis input for BUILD_PLAN revision 3. Date: 2026-11-09. Nothing here is game code; nothing in `reference/` was touched.

**Inputs.** `reference/Roblox_Rebuild_Brief_v2.md` (doc rev 28, captured today; supersedes the PDF), `reference/Roblox_Rebuild_Brief.txt` (the PDF brief), `spec/BRIEF_ADDENDUM.md` (A1-A3; still valid where v2 is silent), `spec/BUILD_PLAN.md` revision 2 (sections 0, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 8, 10 read in full; 1.4, 5, 6.6, 7, 9 skimmed), `spec/roblox_feasibility.md`, the v5.4 subsystem specs where a v2 number had to be checked (`squirrel_abilities_progression.md` for Power, `wildlife_foraging.md` for hollows, `worldgen_*` for lamps, treehouse and trampoline, `traffic.md` for bicycles).

**Method.** A sentence-level diff of v2 against the PDF text (both normalised) yields 240 sentences that exist only in v2. Every one of them is in the inventory below, split into single requirements. Sections 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13 and 16 (= PDF 14) of v2 are word-for-word the PDF; the delta is confined to §3 (hollow trees), §5, §8, the new §13a, the new §14, the new §15 and its Power table. Where v2 repeats the addendum (A1-A3), the item is listed only if v2 changes it.

**How to read the inventory.** *Touches* names plan sections, waves ("W4 #2" = wave 4 item 2), modules (§1.4 names) and decisions ("dec n" = §8 of the plan, "D n" = §2, "inv n" = §3.13, "S n" = W0 spikes). *Covered* is yes / partly / no against revision 2 as written. *Smallest change* is the least edit to the plan that would make the row COVERED. *Conflict* points at the numbered conflicts in section 2 (C1-C22) where the change collides with a standing plan decision.

## 0. Summary (one screen)

- **Inventory:** 229 new or changed requirements (V2-1 to V2-229). Of these the plan already covers **46** (yes), **43** partly, and **140** not at all. The 140 are almost entirely §13a (human life, economy, places and attractions), §14 (camera) and the §15 additions (traffic law for everyone, street lighting, off-road zone, Power).
- **§15 closes the plan's 36 decisions.** 13 are answered explicitly (10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 18, 19, 20, 23, 24, 28, plus the rides/minigames rule and fog in W6); the rest are accepted by the "where a default is not named, accept it" rule, **except** where v2 overrides them indirectly: dec 16 (deer slots 4 -> 5; deer become biteable late), dec 27 (no saving -> saving is now mandatory for purchases), dec 15 (hospital strictly outranks police for an open squirrel slot), dec 20 (Power kept, not dropped). Section 1.5 lists the acceptance map.
- **Twenty-two conflicts with standing plan decisions** (section 2). The structural ones: the 49-slot hard cap versus the §1.5 overflow design (C1); NPC staffing floors of 6 police and 10 hospital staff, plus the new staffed attractions, against the 72-Humanoid hard cap S5 was going to prove (C2); Robux developer products and permanent wardrobes against "no cross-server saving" (C10: a DataStore is now unavoidable); "appearance properties, not tinting" against D22's tint zones (C7); the outbreak cinematic against the end card (C3); first-person vehicles against box-and-ghost vehicles (C13); physics minigames against a plan that deliberately avoided physics (C17); and a world footprint that now includes a hill with a sky lift, a five-storey TV station and an off-road zone beyond the ridge that W6 treated as backdrop (C11).
- **Scope.** The §13a places-and-attractions list alone is roughly **1.5-2x the PDF brief's whole gameplay layer** (the plan's W2 plus W4); with the §8 rule additions, vehicles and traffic law for everyone, the v2 delta is about **2-2.5x the PDF's gameplay work**, and the whole project roughly **doubles** (section 3). Owner time rises from about 7 hours to about 10.5-11.5 hours, mostly the paintball playtest the brief asks for and the per-minigame "ironing-out" sessions the rides/minigames rule implies.
- **Sequencing.** The brief's FRB rule stands (every *role* test-playable); attractions are not roles and most land after the FRB. Nine small items anchor §13a's "full game, not a waiting room" promise in the FRB (section 3.3). Paintball is built in W4 as the brief instructs; the rest of the attractions go into a new parallel pair of waves, **W3c (Track B: attraction lots, interiors, vehicle kits, street lighting) and W4b (Track A: minigame and ride frameworks, items, economy, reputation)**, between W4 and W5. Seven new W0 spikes are needed (S13-S19, section 3.4; S20 is a small check folded into W2), the heaviest being physics-minigame authority, paintball hit detection with lag compensation, and the body budget at the new NPC floors.
- **Owner questions still open:** 24 (section 4); two more (whether animals get the A3 blend, and the ID-verified account) were answered today in Addendum B. The most consequential: whether the in-game-day cadences in v2 (hollow visits every 3 days, paintball passes of 5 days, 5 votes per day) were written knowing a day is 72 real seconds; whether human skin tone may be set as a body-part colour given "not tinting"; whether "credits" and Spicy Dollars are one currency; what a rabid deer does mechanically; and the physics-authority and paintball-venue choices the new W0 spikes will put to G0.

## 2. Conflicts with standing plan decisions

Each conflict names the plan text it collides with, what v2 requires, the recommended resolution and what it costs.

**C1. The 49-slot hard cap versus the §1.5 overflow design (V2-12, V2-17, V2-57, V2-208).** The plan (§1.5, D23, dec 28) serves humans past 35 as "overflow townspeople", never refuses a join, sizes the body budget to serve 55 human players, and tests a 50-player fill (M9). v2 says the server holds 49 players, "when it is full it is full", and "no overflow roles". Resolution: MaxPlayers 49 (owner, one minute on the Creator Hub; the plan's W7 #7 check becomes 49) and Roblox itself makes late joiners wait. **The internal mechanism must stay:** every player joins as a human, so whenever squirrel, rodent, deer or fox slots are unheld the human count exceeds 35 (up to 49) and the posts-and-floors design is what keeps the hospital and station staffed. Rename it (unpromoted humans past the 35 target), drop the 55-human service claim and the "never refused" wording, size the budget for 49 human players plus the new floors (C2), and read "35 of the 43" as 35 + 7 + 1 with the 5 deer and 1 fox making 49. Cost: wording and numbers; no new mechanism.

**C2. NPC staffing floors and the new staffed attractions versus the body budget (V2-20, V2-129, V2-143, V2-159, V2-169, V2-171, V2-176).** The plan's AI floors are 2 police and 3 hospital staff (§1.5), with 62-66 standing human Humanoids and a hard cap of 72 that S5 was going to prove against the community's "about 100 Humanoids" stutter line [feas e]. v2 requires at least 6 NPC police and 10 NPC hospital staff plus ambulance crews that do not count, and adds staffed places: a clinic receptionist, two bartenders, lounge guards, wind-tunnel instructors, a racquetball opponent, steakhouse staff, bowling staff and up to 16 paintball bots. Worst case: 49 human players (all slots held by humans before promotions) + 6 + 10 + 3 crew = 68 before a single shop worker, street walker or attraction NPC; with the plan's street and shop floors and the new posts it is 85-95 Humanoids. Resolution: (a) paintball bots, fender-bender drivers and attraction staff use the D25 pattern (townsperson bodies above their floor re-posted into the venue, never extra bodies); (b) ambulance crews and lounge guards are posts with their own floors; (c) S5 is re-run at the new worst case (about 90 Humanoids) before W1 and sets the hard cap; (d) if it fails, the fallbacks are D4's (lower think rates, Parallel Luau perception, a state stream for Mid/Far bodies) and, new, non-Humanoid rigs for posts that never speak or get bitten (instructors, bartenders behind a counter). The police and hospital floors are the brief's and cannot be cut; street and shop floors can. Cost: S5 redesign plus `population.json`; medium risk.

**C3. Outbreak cinematic versus the end card (V2-25 to V2-30, V2-95, V2-186).** D17 ends the session with one end card and re-deals everyone through possession. v2 wants a cinematic (camera pull-out for every player, time acceleration, squirrels sweeping house to house, overcast and dimming, fade to black at night) that "needs the lamplit town". Resolution: `OutbreakDirector` (server) drives phases on the clock (`Clock.setRate`), flags every squirrel body infected and into an `OutbreakSweep` state, and tells clients when to start; clients run the `Outbreak` camera on the Skyline layer (no streaming requests) and the lighting ramp; the card is the fallback for a client that cannot play it. The cinematic replaces D17's "3 in-game days of outbreak" with a fixed real-time sequence (about 20-30 s), after which the reset happens; the winner still keeps the rodent slot (D17) unless the owner says otherwise (Q12), and infected squirrels alive at the win keep squirrel bodies (V2-95). Dependency: lamps must exist before the W4 build for the sequence to be judged, so greybox street lighting moves to W3c (C16). Cost: medium (one director, one camera mode, one lighting state, one brain state).

**C4. Infection reset on rodent death versus per-actor persistence (V2-32, V2-35, V2-36).** The plan keeps per-actor infection across rodent tenures (D17; the killer's 30 % rule uses a per-rodent tally). v2 wipes infection to the new rodent alone when a rodent dies, cures humans (instantly if recently bitten or en route), and drops infected squirrels to lowest-rank human. Consequence the owner should know: the hidden win now has to be built inside one rodent tenure, which makes it much rarer than the plan's default; that appears to be intended ("the board is not wiped" refers to hoards, points and reputation). Resolution: `InfectionService.resetToRodent()` on `rodent.died` plus two Ladder rows; `RodentLoop` and `LadderE2E` gain the case. Cost: small.

**C5. Health drain versus regeneration (V2-41, V2-49, V2-51, V2-187).** v2 adds a drain of 1 % per in-game day without a bite and heals of 10 % (human) and 20 % (squirrel) per bite, and keeps "regenerates slowly while sneaking or resting in a hollow". The plan's defaults (dec 12) regenerate 2 hp/s while stalking and 5 hp/s in a hollow: a full 55-point kick heals in 27 s of stalking, while the drain removes 1 hp per 72 real seconds, so the drain could never matter and the bite heals would be redundant. Resolution: keep all three as Tunables but lower the regen defaults so the drain and the bite heals are the main recovery paths (proposed: 0.25 hp/s stalking, 1 hp/s in a hollow, so a kick takes about a minute in cover), and keep the two clocks separate as v2 says (Power on real active minutes, health on in-game days). The alert-zone strength boost (V2-41) is a kick multiplier that must stay below 1.82 or one kick kills; default 1.25. Cost: tunables and one Lune test.

**C6. The human rank formula and the currency (V2-61, V2-82, V2-97, V2-101 to V2-103, V2-198).** Brief §6 (unchanged): "base rank among humans = time logged in ... modified by job credits"; §13 (unchanged): "credits raise rank ... and buy the station upgrades". §13a (new): currency is Spicy Dollars; "queue rank = money plus reputation"; "money's main use is buying your place in the human queue only, never into police or hospital"; rank "inside that administration" for duty; 10 in-game days before being ranked. These are reconcilable on one reading: there is **one currency** (Spicy Dollars; "credits" in §6/§13 and "coins" in dec 20 are the same thing), the **human queue** is scored by time served + Spicy Dollars + reputation (+ entourage, kicks, quest points), the **tier-2 ranks** (hospital, police) are scored by performance only (money never buys a place there), and station upgrades are still bought with Spicy Dollars from the pooled fund. The alternative reading (two currencies: credits for rank, Spicy Dollars for shopping) is listed as Q3. Resolution: `Ladder.score(human)` and `Ladder.score(hospital|police)` split; `Economy/Credits` becomes `Economy/Wallet` in cents; the 10-day gate as `Ladder.params.rankGateDays`. Cost: small in code, medium in design (a Lune economy simulation: at 2 pennies per in-game hour, minimum wage is 0.48 S$ per 72-second day, so 1 Robux = 4 S$ buys about 8 in-game days of wages; the owner should see that curve before prices are final).

**C7. "Appearance properties scripts can set, not tinting" versus D22 and D13 (V2-64, V2-65; Addendum B1).** D22 puts skin and two accent colours on humans as `MeshPart.Color` showing through transparent texels of a colour texture; D13 takes two accent colours from the avatar. v2 §13a drops the accents entirely (face, hair, skin tone only) and forbids tinting for humans; Addendum B1 keeps the A3 animal blend (capped fur tint, pattern colours, one rigid accessory). Resolution: humans take skin tone as body-part colour (the same scriptable property Roblox's own `BodyColors` uses; the owner's objection is to tinting textures, so this should be confirmed in one line), the face decal and a hair or head accessory; outfits become plain colour textures with no tint zones (simpler than D22; fallback (a) of D22 is no longer needed for accents). S8(e) still runs for the skin texels. Animals keep S8(c2) (runtime `SurfaceAppearance.Color`) because B1 keeps the fur tint. Traceability rows A3-6 (accents) become DROPPED by v2. Cost: a simplification.

**C8. "Otherwise only police drive" versus golf carts, bicycles and the top-two humans' pickups and SUVs (V2-66, V2-216, V2-218).** The plan has exactly two player vehicles (patrol cars, police only; §1.5 Devices, D10). v2 adds carts and bicycles for everyone, police carts (joyriding), 6 pickups and 4 SUVs for the top two ranked humans, a jet ski and a gondola, and says vehicles switch to first person with modelled interiors. Resolution: generalise `PlayerCars` to `Vehicles/PlayerVehicles` with per-kind motors (`CarMotor` parameter sets for car, cart, pickup, SUV; `BikeMotor` with balance-free arcade handling; `JetSkiMotor`), a `Ladder.subroles.humanDriver` (two slots, top two by human rank, hand-over when parked), and an `anyone` kind for carts and bikes. Open: whether carts and bikes are in the fatal swept fleet and whether they count as "driving" (Q8); default: carts are in the fleet at low speed and can earn the kill credit, bikes are not. Cost: large (motors, interiors, 7 vehicle kits, traffic law for players, C14).

**C9. Humans killing infected squirrels; guards killing squirrels (V2-90 to V2-93, V2-169).** The plan has no human attack on squirrels and squirrels have no health; brief §6 (unchanged) says a regular squirrel cannot kill the rodent and an infected squirrel "loses heavily and drops in the queue". v2 adds: kicks land on any infected squirrel; killing an infected non-leader squirrel kills it, the body respawns healthy, the top hospital player is promoted into it and the killed player goes to the bottom of the human queue; lounge guards "annihilate any squirrel". Resolution: infected squirrels gain health and are valid strike targets; healthy squirrels stay immune to human strikes (the brief names only infected ones); three Ladder rows; the outcome for a healthy player squirrel killed by a lounge guard is open (Q6; default: same recycle rule, since the guard "annihilates"). Cost: small-medium.

**C10. Robux developer products and permanent wardrobes versus "no cross-server saving" (V2-98, V2-110, V2-165, V2-167, V2-200; dec 27, D14).** The plan keeps all state per server and needs no DataStore before W7 (optional). v2 sells Spicy Dollars for Robux and sells garb "kept permanently". Anything bought with Robux must survive a server shutdown or it is a chargeback and a policy problem, so a **DataStore profile is now mandatory**: Spicy-Dollar balance (at least the purchased part; simpler to save the whole wallet), wardrobe (paintball garb, fashion-stand outfits), receipt ledger for idempotent `ProcessReceipt`, and, cheaply alongside, reputation, lifetime stats and the "once per player" water-gun flag if it is lifetime (Q11). Rank and homes stay per server as the brief scopes them. Owner steps: create the developer products on the Creator Hub (about 10 minutes; Open Cloud has no developer-product creation endpoint we have verified, so this is an owner web task), and in Studio turn on **Game Settings -> Security -> Enable Studio Access to API Services** (2 minutes) so Studio playtests can read the store. `Persistence/Profiles` moves from W7-optional to **W4** (before any product is live) with ProfileStore-style session locking, and spike S15 proves the loop in W0. Cost: medium; it also adds the first real data-loss risk to the project (mitigated by the receipt ledger and a Lune-tested serialiser).

**C11. The world footprint (V2-149, V2-194, V2-206, V2-214).** The plan's world is the v5.4 rectangle (about 4,350 x 2,520 st), `World/Height` is a verbatim port, and everything beyond is W6 backdrop bands at 0.5-2 km. v2 adds a hill with a sky lift and hilltop restaurant (a second vantage), a five-storey TV station, several large lots, and an off-road zone beyond a ridge with real trees, a beach that runs past the public area and a back field with hills, where "detail is high on the ridge". Resolution: `World/Height` keeps the verbatim v5.4 function inside the v5.4 rectangle and adds authored extensions outside it (hill, ridge, back field) from `data/town/terrain_extensions.json`; the world clamp and districts grow; S6 (bake and terrain time) and S11 (drone far view) run on the larger footprint; the near backdrop band moves outward. The hill also needs a gondola cable and cabins (a `Ride`). Cost: medium on Track B; S6/S11 re-scope.

**C12. Paintball as an instanced arena in the server versus a separate place (V2-150 to V2-164).** Options: (a) a separate place through `TeleportService` with reserved servers, or (b) an instanced match inside the TV station lot in the town server. (a) breaks the possession model at both ends (the body is released on leave and re-possessed on return, rank and wallet must round-trip through a DataStore or `TeleportData`), isolates the arena's CPU cost, and gives a clean 8v8 budget; (b) keeps one server, one body, one wallet, bots as re-posted townspeople (C2), and the arena's cost (16 projectiles in flight, hit rewinds, bot brains across five floors) inside the town server's 4 ms AI budget plus the physics of 16 unanchored players. Recommendation: **(b)**, because the brief's own rules (one actor model, 5-day access passes on the game clock, garb worn back into town) assume continuity with the town, and because (a) needs persistence that is only now entering the plan; (a) stays the fallback if S18 shows the arena cannot share the server. Lag compensation moves from W7 to W4 for the arena only (pose history for players is cheap; vehicles stay W7). Bots: the D25 pattern. The brief's gate ("if the mechanics are not good it gets cut") becomes G-PB at OS-5b. Cost: large (an FPS mode) but bounded by the brief's "generic marker, mechanics first".

**C13. First-person requirements versus box-and-ghost vehicles and the v5.4 stalk camera (V2-54, V2-178 to V2-183).** The plan renders AI vehicles as ghosts from a pose stream and builds player cars to the logical box (D9, D10, §5.2); the rodent's stalk is first person as in v5.4 (FRB R2). v2 wants humans to be able to go first person anywhere, vehicles and the gondola to switch to first person automatically with modelled interiors and dashboards, paintball and lounges locked to first person, and animals in third person. Resolution: a camera-inside-a-kinematic-vehicle spike (S14: no clipping, no judder at 60 Hz with `AlignPosition`, HUD inside the dash), six vehicle interiors in P3, interior kits held to an eye-level bar in W5, zone-locked first person; the stalk becomes an over-shoulder third-person stalk unless the owner keeps v5.4's as an exception (Q10). The forward-locked squirrel-chase camera (V2-54) is a third-person lock and fits §14. Cost: medium art, small code, one spike.

**C14. Everyone obeys traffic law, including police, and pedestrians can be hit (V2-189 to V2-191).** dec 14's defaults: people, deer and the fox are protected by yielding (so only squirrels are ever hit); violations exist for AI cars only. v2: every player vehicle is subject to speeding, stop-sign and parking rules; an officer who hits a pedestrian loses the car, sits in a cell and is demoted; the pedestrian wakes in a hospital bed. Resolution: `TrafficService.violations` evaluates player vehicles too; AI officers ticket players (a fine in Spicy cents, a quest for player officers), repeat offenders and pedestrian hits go to a cell (a timed state in the station's W3a #4 cells) with the Ladder demotion rule for police; `SweptCollision` gains a non-fatal human case for player-driven vehicles (knockdown, ambulance, admitted as an uninfected trauma patient); AI vehicles keep yielding to people. The off-road district is exempt (V2-215). Cost: medium; it also makes the §3.9 police quest catalogue apply to players as targets.

Cost: small.
