# Bowling, the new feel: what to look at (10 to 15 minutes)

You said bowling felt "simple, crude, and not very fun", with "no way to put any angle or twist", that the aiming "wasn't very good", and that there was "no room for the character to walk in between the lanes". This is what changed. It has all been tested without a screen, so the feel is yours to judge.

## What changed
- **Aiming like a real bowler.** You choose where you **stand** on the foul line and which **arrow** on the lane the ball rolls over. The line through those two is your angle, so you can now throw across the lane, not only straight. A glowing mark on the foul line shows where the ball leaves your hand, a glowing chevron sits on your target arrow, and a dotted line shows the ball's path over the arrows and on to where the lane starts to grip.
- **Hook (the twist).** Four steps each way, from "a touch of" hook up to "full". It works like a real lane: the front of the lane is oiled (it shines a little), so the ball slides almost straight there. The back of the lane is dry, so the spin grips and the ball curls. Once it has curled it rolls straight on. A slow ball curls more, a fast one less.
- **Hook into the pocket for strikes.** A ball that curls into the pocket (just right of the front pin for a right-hander) keeps driving through the pins and strikes far more often than a straight ball. In my test throws, with the small misses a real person makes: a full hook into the pocket struck about 8 times in 10, a straight ball into the pocket about 5 in 10, a straight ball at the front pin about 1 in 12. A straight ball still works if you hit the pocket exactly.
- **Left-handers too.** Stand on the left and hook right into the other pocket. It works just as well.
- **The approach.** When you let go of the power button, your person walks four steps, swings the ball back and through, slides on one foot and lets go at the line. Others see you walk up and slide. Only you see the arm swing.
- **You can see the spin.** Each lane's ball has a thumb hole and two finger holes, so you can see it turn. A hooking ball turns on a tilted axis.
- **The pins.** They still scatter and knock each other over. A fallen pin no longer lies through a pin that is still standing, or through the side wall. Where pins pile up, one rests on top of another.
- **Room to walk.** The seats behind each pair of lanes are a little narrower, so there is a clear aisle about two body-widths wide between every pair. It runs from the main walkway down to the lanes. Behind the ball racks you can walk along from lane 1 to lane 18. You never need to jump. The squirrels' tunnel up through the ball return is unchanged.

## How to play
1. Walk up to a lane and press **E**, as before.
2. On your turn:
   - **A / D** (or the D-pad): step left and right along the line.
   - **Mouse**, **Left / Right** arrows or the **left stick**: move your target arrow.
   - **Z / X**, the **mouse wheel** or **LB / RB**: hook left or right. The bar under the meter shows how much.
   - **Hold Space**, the mouse button or **R2**: the power meter runs. **Let go in the green**: your person walks up and bowls.
   - On a phone: **Step**, **Aim** and **Hook** buttons, and **HOLD TO BOWL**.
3. The card on the left explains it all. **H** hides it.

A first strike ball for a right-hander (the status line at the bottom names your board and arrow): step right with **D** until it says **stand board 13**, aim at the **2nd arrow from the right**, press **Z** four times for **full hook left**, and let go in the green. Then adjust like a real bowler: if the ball arrives left of the front pin, step a board left; if it arrives right, step a board right.

## Please check
| # | What to look at | What you should see |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The aim marks | A glowing mark on the foul line where the ball starts, a gold chevron on your target arrow, and dots running over both down the lane. They move as you step and aim |
| 2 | Angle | Stand on one side and aim at an arrow on the other side: the ball really crosses the lane |
| 3 | Hook | The ball goes nearly straight for the first two thirds of the lane, then curls, then rolls on straight. Full hook curls about 1.5 to 2 studs. A slow ball curls more |
| 4 | Strikes | Hooking into the pocket should feel rewarding: more strikes than throwing straight. Straight at the front pin should leave splits |
| 5 | The approach | Four steps, the swing back and through, a slide, and the ball leaves the hand at the line. Does it look natural, and is it the right speed (0.8 seconds)? |
| 6 | Spin on the ball | The finger holes turn as the ball rolls, on a tilt for a hook ball |
| 7 | Fallen pins | No pin lying through a standing pin or through the side wall. Pins in a pile rest on each other |
| 8 | Walking | From the main walkway, walk between the seats of two pairs down to any lane, and along behind the ball racks to another lane, without jumping |
| 9 | Phone and gamepad | Every control works on touch and with a gamepad |
| 10 | Sounds | The roll sound (your uploaded one) as the ball goes, the clatter of pins, the pinsetter |

## Costs
- Parts: 54 small parts for the finger holes (3 on each of the 18 balls). The narrower seats cost nothing. The alley is now 8,039 parts (the limit is 8,100).
- On your screen while it is your turn: 5 glowing marks and up to about 20 dots, as before.
- Each throw is worked out once by the server, as before. The spin adds a little to what each throw sends (about a third more numbers for the ball's path).
