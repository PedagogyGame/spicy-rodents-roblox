not just a single place and point where branches come out of and all split, bloom out ... edit the branches of about

## What changed

**Ten trees have new branches** (12 % of the 83 trees that are not hollow; the hollow trees were left alone, their
insides are being reworked separately). Each keeps every branch it had and gains four to six more that leave the
trunk at different heights: two **low** branches a squirrel can reach from the lawn (they start 7 to 13 studs up),
one or two **middle** ones, and on most a **high** one up in the crown. Every new branch is climbable like the old
ones: climb the trunk, press A or D to pick a branch (the low ones come up first as you climb), hold W to go onto it.
You can also leap at a low branch from the grass and press E in the air to grab it.

## How the new branches look, now and later

- **Right now** the new branches on the ten trees are drawn as plain bark with a few round leafy clumps, because the
  tree models you see were made before they grew. They are solid and climbable.
- **New tree models** for the ten trees, with the new branches grown into the bark and leaves like the rest, are built
  and checked, waiting for the lead to upload them (we never upload). Once uploaded, the game swaps them in by itself
  and hides the plain stand-ins.
- The three new trees already wear finished art.

## What it costs (phones)

- Parts: the whole town is **26,367** parts (budget 27,000; it was 26,827 before: the invisible collision pieces
  inside every tree were made from fewer, longer pieces, which made room).
- The ten trees' stand-in branches: 539 parts until the new models are uploaded.
- The three new trees: 195 parts and about 51,000 triangles (the same as the trees they copy).
- The ten rebuilt tree models, once uploaded: about 11,000 triangles more than the ten models they replace
  (123,190 -> 134,775; every one within the per-tree budget of 8,000 bark and 6,000 leaf triangles). Their low
  branches carry leaves too, so the crowns come down in tiers instead of one ball on a pole.
