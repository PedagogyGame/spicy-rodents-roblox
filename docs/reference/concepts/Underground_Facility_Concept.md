# SPICY RODENT — FUTURE CLAUDE VERSION
## Underground Facility — Concept Note
**Status:** Concept reference only. Not an existing game, implementation request, or approved production specification.
**Purpose:** Preserve the user's visual and atmospheric direction for possible future development.

### Core Vision
A large underground complex is built around **wide, light-brown tunnels**. The tunnels are substantial, navigable spaces, not cramped mine shafts. They contain and connect mining equipment, living quarters, stores, entertainment areas, and laboratories.

The facility should feel like a place where people actually live and work. Its daily routine is ordinary to those inside it: players move casually through the complex, mining and doing chores as part of normal life. The unusual scale and hidden infrastructure are present without everyone constantly reacting to them.

### Spaces and Environment
- **Main tunnels:** Broad passageways with light-brown earth or rock surfaces, large enough to make the facility feel expansive and functional.
- **Mining areas:** Equipment and ongoing mining activity integrated into everyday operations.
- **Living quarters:** Residential spaces connected to the working complex.
- **Stores:** Retail or supply areas that make the facility feel inhabited.
- **Entertainment areas:** Places for recreation and downtime.
- **Laboratories:** More restricted spaces, often concealed from casual view behind advanced doors or tinted glass.

These functions should coexist within one believable underground environment rather than appearing as unrelated levels.

### Doors, Security, and Visibility
Two important door types establish the contrast between ordinary daily life and restricted operations:
1. **High-tech sliding doors** with keycard access.
2. **Heavy vault-style doors** marking especially secure spaces.

Laboratory interiors and staff are often only partly visible. Tinted glass, closed doors, and restricted access communicate that more is happening behind the scenes without requiring explicit exposition.

### NPC Presence
Laboratory NPCs wear **dark suits or lab coats**. Most remain unseen, glimpsed through tinted glass, or encountered only briefly in controlled areas. Their presence should suggest ongoing research and administration, not constant dramatic encounters.

The larger facility should feel occupied and operational, with ordinary movement and work continuing around the player.

### Employee Structure
**Two distinct employee classes** operate behind the scenes.

Their names, responsibilities, authority, appearance, relationship to one another, and gameplay significance have **not yet been specified**. Preserve the distinction without inventing those details.

### Player Experience and Tone
- Players move through the facility **casually**.
- **Mining and daily chores are normal**, routine activities.
- The underground settlement feels inhabited, practical, and self-sustaining.
- A subtle contrast exists between public/common spaces and less-visible, controlled laboratory operations.
- Mystery, if developed later, should arise naturally from architecture, access restrictions, and glimpses of activity—not from prematurely imposed plot twists.

### Boundaries for Future Development
This note does **not** establish:
- A game engine, platform, map size, or technical architecture.
- The identity or duties of the two employee classes.
- A storyline, conspiracy, combat system, or mission structure.
- Specific access permissions, progression systems, or economic mechanics.
- A requirement to start building the game now.
